Overview
The DC will be updating its speaker scoring system across all DC endorsed
tournaments. This amendment is intended to align the Sri Lankan debating
circuit with the World Schools Debating Championship (WSDC) standards.
The primary objectives for this change are to make external WSDC resources
and training materials directly applicable to the local circuit, and to create
more accountability and transparency when scoring speeches.
Accordingly, the following standards will be enforced:
1. Adoption of WSDC Scoring Range: the speaker scoring system will shift
from the traditional 69–81 range to the WSDC standard 60–80 range.
(See Guideline 1 for the scale mapping and comparison of the two
scales)
2. Enforcement of Component Scoring: adjudicators will be required to
allocate individual scores for the three distinct elements of a speech,
weighted as follows:
○ Content: 40% of the score
○ Style: 40% of the score
○ Strategy: 20% of the score
(See Guideline 2 for a detailed breakdown of each scoring element).
3. Component Point Allocation: Under the new 60–80 scale, the points for
each component will be allocated within the following ranges:
○ Content: 24–32 points
○ Style: 24–32 points
○ Strategy: 12–16 points
(See Guideline 3 for an understanding of how to score each component)
This amendment will come into effect on 1 January 2026 for all DC endorsed
tournaments

Guideline 2: Speaker Score Element Definitions
WSDC Speaker Scores are evaluated using 3 distinct, discrete elements:
1. Content (40% of the score)
● Deals with WHAT is being presented.
● Evaluates the quality of content, how well it is proved, and the
evaluation of all levels of analysis to prove the claim.
● Covers arguments, rebuttal, content of Points of Information (POIs),
and responses to POIs.
● If an argument or rebuttal is weak or poorly developed, it is generally a
content weakness.
2. Style (40% of the score)
● Deals with HOW the content is presented.
● Includes: pace of speech, volume and tonal variations, choice of
vocabulary (too technical or too lay; emotive or dry?), eye contact, and
body language (if applicable).
● Does not include natural aspects of the person’s voice or body
language (e.g., accents).
● Style also includes what content is used and how claims are explained.
If rhetoric and good examples are present, it would be considered
good style.
● Bad style typically hinders the intelligibility or persuasiveness of the
argumentation offered, and could include mumbling, shouting, or
speaking too quickly to be understood.
3. Strategy (20% of the score)
● Deals with WHY content is said.
● It is the sum of choices that a speaker makes in order to win
● It includes: interpretation and relevance of the motion, time
allocation, structuring of the speech (prioritization), consistency
between arguments and speeches, and dealing with POIs within your
speech.
● Strategy points are awarded when a speaker identifies and addresses
the right issues in the debate and makes intelligent calls in relation to
said strategy.Good strategy can be independent of good content and
is intrinsically tied to good engagement.
● Points can be cut for not taking POIs (if offered). Limited at a
maximum of 1 point deduction.
