Tabulation	Procedures	for	all	Debaters’	Council	Endorsed	Tournaments	
(WSDC	Format)
1.	General
1.1 The	procedures	detailed	in	this	document	have	been	approved	by	the	Board	of	
Directors	of	the	Debaters’	Council	of	Sri	Lanka
1.2	Procedures	detailed	in	this	document	are	mandatory at	all	Debaters’	Council	(DC)	
endorsed	tournaments	in	the	WSDC	format	from	October	2020.	
1.3	Tabulation	for	all	DC	endorsed	tournaments	will	be	done	through	an online	
tabulation	software approved	by	the	Tournament	Committee	of the	Debaters’	Council.
2.	Creating	the	Draw	for	Preliminary	Rounds
2.1 Preliminary Round 1 shall be randomly drawn. Teams from the same institution will
not be drawn against each other in the first preliminary round.
2.2 For all subsequent preliminary rounds, the draw shall be power-paired using an
approved computer tabulation programme, administered by the Tab Masters.
2.3 At the end of each preliminary round, from preliminary round 1 to the last, a league
table shall be calculated by the computer tabulation programme for the purpose of
preparing the draw for the next round, ranking teams based on the following criteria in
this order or priority:
(i) The number of debates which the team has won, followed by
(ii) The total speaker score of the team, followed by
(iii)	 The	net	margin	of victory,	for the	team		
2.4 When creating the draw for each preliminary round from round 2 onwards, teams
will be divided into brackets made up of teams which have won the same number of
debates over the preliminary rounds that have already been completed.
2.5 Each team will be drawn to debate against another team from the same bracket
as them.		Teams shall not debate against each other more than once during the
preliminary rounds. If a bracket contains an odd number of teams, a ‘pull-up’ is
necessary before generating the draw for the bracket. For ‘pull-up’ principles, refer to
2.6-2.10.
2.6 When a bracket consists of an odd number of teams, a ‘pull-up’ is necessary to
ensure that every team in the bracket has an opponent from the same bracket. The
computer tabulation programme shall determine the ‘pull-up’ teams from the top
bracket down to the bottom, based on guidelines specified in 2.5, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9 and 2.10.
2.7 If it is necessary for a team to be ‘pulled-up’, the team which is ‘pulled-up’ shall be
taken from the bracket immediately below (or the closest available bracket below in
the event that all teams in the bracket immediately below have either been ‘pulledup’ already, or have competed against all teams in the higher bracket which needs a
‘pull-up’ team).
2.8 Teams shall be ‘pulled-up’ to a higher bracket no more than once during
preliminary rounds, unless no other teams could be pulled-up without violating
guidelines specified in 2.5, 2.7, 2.8, or 2.9. Each bracket shall contain no more than
one team that is ‘pulled-up’ from the lower brackets (with the exception of the
extreme case specified in 2.10).
2.9 The team which is ‘pulled-up’ to a bracket shall be the team in the middle of the
bracket immediately below (or the closest available bracket below as explained in 2.7).
In the event that pulling-up a team would violate 2.5 (avoid repeated matchup) or 2.8
(‘pull-up’ no more than once), the team that has the next lowest average rank in the
preliminary round league tables shall be ‘pulled-up’ instead.
2.10 In the very extreme case (<<1%) where there is an odd number of teams in the
bottom bracket and all teams in the bottom bracket have already competed against all
teams in the bracket immediately above them, a team needs to be ‘pulled-up’ from the
bottom bracket to the third lowest bracket in order to meet the guidelines specified in
2.5 (avoid repeated matchup). If the third lowest bracket originally requires a ‘pull-up’
team, the ‘pull-up’ team shall come from the bottom bracket instead of the second
lowest bracket; If the third lowest bracket does not need a ‘pull-up’ team before
‘pulling-up’ a team from the bottom bracket, another ‘pull-up’ needs to be made from
the second lowest bracket based on guidelines specified in 2.9.
2.11 Once all brackets contain an even number of teams, the computer tabulation
software shall rank all teams based on the following criteria in this order or priority:
(i) The average judges’ scores awarded to the team, followed by
(ii) The net margin of victory in favour of the team.
Note:		When calculating the ranking within the bracket for the purpose of
power-paired draw, number of wins shall no longer matter, as all teams in the
bracket shall be treated as being on the same number of wins.
2.12 The computer tabulation programme shall pair teams up in the following system,
based on the bracket ranking calculated as instructed in 2.11:
(a)		Highest-ranked team within the bracket against lowest-ranked team within
the bracket,
(b)		2nd highest-ranked team within the bracket against 2nd lowest-ranked team
within the bracket, etc.
Adjustment to the above system is only allowed for provisions specified in 2.5 (avoid
repeated matchup), 2.13 and 2.14. The principle for adjustment is that it shall keep the
highest [Prop ranking within bracket + Opp ranking within bracket] among all matches
in the bracket as close as possible to [Number of teams in the bracket + 1].
2.13		As far as possible, the computer tabulation programme shall ensure that as many
teams as possible shall have balanced side allocation across the preliminary rounds.
However, where necessary, the computer tabulation programme shall be able to assign
some teams to have 4 debates on 1 side and 1 debate on the other side in order to meet
other provisions within these guidelines.
3. Procedures regarding walkovers and swing teams
3.1		In the event that the number of competing teams is an odd number, the Host shall
arrange for a ‘swing team’ debate in the preliminary rounds in order to ensure an even
number of teams.
3.2 The swing team shall be made up of 3 students who shall meet the age criteria and
education status criteria for debaters set out in the DC League Rules, however the
members of the swing team do not necessarily have to come from the same institution.
3.3 Adjudicators shall judge the swing team in the same way as a competing team in the
tournament, however the swing team and its members shall not be considered to be an
official competing team and thus shall not be eligible to qualify for the break rounds or
receive any team or individual speaker awards.
3.4 In the event of a team being unable to participate in a round, the following shall
happen:
(i) The team’s opponents in the draw for that round shall be awarded a 6 point
margin victory along with (for the purpose of rankings at the end of the
preliminary rounds) a team score which is equivalent to that team’s average
score for the preliminary rounds, rounded down to the nearest 0.5, in which the
team did participate, unless the Chief Adjudicators decide that it is possible to
amend the draw for the round to ensure that every competing team
participating in the round has an appropriate opponent (for example by adding
or withdrawing the swing team) in a manner which, in the Chief Adjudicators’
opinion, does not significantly affect the fairness and integrity of the draw.
(ii) The team which is unable to participate in the round shall be awarded a 6
point defeat for the round along with (for the purpose of rankings at the end of
the preliminary rounds) a team score adjusted to reflect a 6 point loss based on
the average score of the opposition.
3.5 In the event of a walkover in the first preliminary round and the organizers are
unable to field/withdraw a swing team to accomodate a debate:
(i) The team receiving the walkover will be awarded a 6 point margin victory for
the round along with (for the purpose of rankings at the end of the preliminary
rounds) an average speaker score of 75 for substantive speeches, and a 37.5 for
the reply speech.
(ii) The team unable to participate in the round shall be awarded a 6 point defeat
for the round along with (for the purposes of rankings at the end of the
preliminary rounds) an average speaker of 73 for substantive speeches, and a
37.5 for the reply speech.
Overview
The	above-mentioned	criteria	can	be	summarised	as	follows:
• All	tabulation	must	take	place	through	an	approved	tabulation	software
Round	1:	
- Random	draw	(teams	from	the	same	institution	should	not	meet)
All	subsequent	preliminary	rounds:
- Teams	to	be	ordered	based	on	wins,	total	speaker	points,	margins
- Teams	to	be	divided	into	brackets	based	on	same	number	of	wins
- Draw	will	be	based	on	a	within	the	bracket	fold	(highest	vs	lowest)
- No	two	teams	will	meet	each	other	more	than	once
Pull	ups	(where	required):
- A	team	from	the	middle	of	the	bracket	below	will	be	pulled	up	
- No	team	will	be	pulled	up	twice
Walkovers:
- Organizers	 should	 attempt	 to	 field	 a	 “swing	 team”	 as	 much	 as	 possible.	 A	
walkover	should	always	be	considered	a	last	resort
- Team	giving	 the	walkover	will	 receive	an	automatic	loss	 (automatic	win	 for	 the	
team	receiving	the	walkover)
- The	win/loss	margin	will	be	6 points
- If	 walkover	 takes	 place	 in	 the	 first	 round,	 speakers	 from	 the	 team	 giving	 the	
walkover	 will	 be	 given	an	automatic	 73;	 Speakers	 from	 the	 team	 receiving	 the	
walkover	will	be	given	an	automatic	75
